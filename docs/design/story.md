# Story and world

Status: agreed premise with a working story treatment. See [decisions](decisions.md).

## Agreed direction

- Space setting with exploration, collecting, and repeated visits at its heart.
- One continuing player character/class across a progressive campaign.
- Hunt the scattered remains of a lost spacefaring civilization. Recovering and
  assembling discoveries gradually reveals an ancient navigation network.
- A megacorporation seeks galactic control and opposes the player.
- The corporation keeps rebuilding the player because they pay for the service
  and the CEO needs a Skavengar to open the last planet.
- Its concealed leader is known as **the CEO**, taking inspiration from the
  recurring unseen menace of Inspector Gadget's Claw. Build an original
  personality and presentation around that idea.
- Capture the excitement of treasure hunting and gun discovery without copying
  Borderlands' vault premise, characters, or setting.
- A mysterious female guide communicates with and helps the player for reasons
  they do not understand. **Echo** is her working name.
- Open directly on the first world: the player wakes somewhere unfamiliar. Teach
  controls through play, building toward fast action and collecting the first relic.

## Working premise

The chosen title is **Skavengar**. As working world terminology, Skavengars are
independent explorers who descend into planetary ruins to recover relics and
salvage. The corporation calls them trespassers; remote settlements depend on
what they bring back. Their class is their specialty within that profession.

Possible CEO line:

> You're a Skavengar. You find things. We own things.

You wake on an unfamiliar world, unsure where you are. Survival and a mysterious
voice draw you toward your first relic, beginning an effort to understand and
restore a lost network connecting distant worlds. Artifacts are functional pieces
of that network. The salvage-explorer identity and battered ship remain part of
the working campaign premise; how you arrived and how you reach the ship are open.

The collection gradually becomes a working machine. An apparently inert object
responds when another component arrives. Completed devices reveal coordinates,
restore functions, or give access to new expedition sites.

The corporation wants exclusive control of this network. Whoever controls travel
can control trade, supplies, and whether a settlement remains connected at all.
Exploration and collecting directly advance the conflict: the player restores
access to places the corporation intends to own.

## The corporation and the CEO

**Meridian Industries** is a working name, not final. It supplies colonies, builds
ships, manufactures weapons, and operates existing jump routes. Its public promise
is dependable infrastructure; its contracts make communities dependent on it.

The CEO's proposed defining belief: everything belongs to someone, and eventually
that someone should be them. They regard an unexplored planet as an unregistered
asset and ancient technology as property awaiting acquisition.

Presentation proposals:

- Corporate terminals, billboards, intercepted calls, and defeated security robots
  carry their messages. A recognizable three-note company chime announces them.
- Their image is obscured: a backlit office, cropped conference feed, or company
  logo replacing the camera. Exact appearance and identity remain undecided.
- Their threats sound like polite customer service. Small breaks in composure
  signal real anger.
- At first the player is a contractor number. Later the CEO knows their name,
  ship, and prized discoveries.
- Show consequences: supply restrictions, seized finds, occupied landing sites,
  and communities trapped by contracts. Humor should coexist with a credible threat.

Voice samples, not final dialogue:

> Congratulations on your discovery. Our discovery.

> Your landing permit has been revoked. Please remain on the planet.

> We have acquired your outstanding debt. We would prefer not to acquire your next of kin.

A visible regional director could command field operations and provide recurring
confrontations. **Director Voss** was an earlier villain-name proposal; it could
be used for this subordinate, but neither that character nor the name is settled.
Do not identify Voss as the CEO by default.

## Corporate reconstruction

**Agreed:** when the player dies, the corporation rebuilds their body and returns
them to the ship with everything in their loadout. It collects a percentage of
their money for the service. See [death rules](play-loop.md) for unresolved details.

This makes corporate dependence part of ordinary play: even an independent
Skavengar pays the corporation to keep going. Possible presentation is a short,
cheerful service receipt showing the fee and remaining balance.

Sample service message, not final dialogue:

> Body restored. Personal effects retained. Thank you for your continued business.

**Agreed motive:** the player pays the corporation, and the CEO needs a Skavengar
to open the last planet. Reconstruction keeps that useful explorer moving toward
the CEO's objective while generating revenue. The service's continued operation
is intentional, not a bureaucratic oversight.

Corporate opposition must fit that motive. Retrieval teams, surveillance, and
attempts to control discoveries can serve the plan; their specific orders and
knowledge remain open. Establish why each confrontation helps corporate interests
instead of making the CEO's need for the player meaningless.

Still decide how reconstruction works before the player reaches the ship and
after the last planet opens. The wake-up scene is not automatically a reconstruction
event. A late story reveal must account for the player's ongoing death/retry loop.

## Why the CEO needs a Skavengar

**Agreed:** a Skavengar must open the last planet for the corporation. The exact
access mechanism and reason corporate personnel cannot do it remain open.

**Working proposal:** Echo can authorize ancient machinery but cannot physically
reach and repair its scattered interfaces. The player can reach them, recover
components, and reconnect them, but needs her guidance and authorization. Their
cooperation gradually restores a route to the last planet. The CEO follows those
activations and intends to take control once the route is open.

In this version, the player is a capable explorer Echo chooses to trust. The story
does not require a special bloodline or a particular playable class. The first
relic could establish their portable connection. None of this access mechanism
is finalized yet.

Questions to resolve before committing to this explanation:

- Why does Echo trust this explorer, and what prevents coercing or replacing them?
- Why can't corporate reconstruction copy or extract the authorization?
- What can the CEO observe, and when do they learn about Echo?
- Does opening the planet expose something Echo also wants released?

## Echo: the mysterious guide

**Agreed role:** a woman communicates with the player, offers useful help, and has
an undisclosed reason for doing so. Her siren-like presence draws the player deeper.
This is an original character; no Borderlands Siren lore or class system is implied.

**Working treatment:** Echo reaches the player through ancient-network devices.
She knows passages, defenses, and machinery, but asks unexpectedly basic questions
about surface life and the sky. Her first intervention saves the player before she
asks for anything. Trust grows through practical help and personal attention.

Proposed roles during expeditions:

- Warn about a specific hazard, then let the player act on that warning.
- Point out optional signals leading to caches, survivors, or fragments she wants.
- Become clearer and more conversational closer to a planetary core.
- React to collected objects with recognition, emotion, or incomplete memories.
- Occasionally ask the player to leave a find behind without fully explaining why.
- Remember close calls and check whether the player returned safely.

**Possible secret, not finalized:** her consciousness was distributed through the
network when it shut down. Activating cores restores her memories and influence,
and might allow her to escape. She cares about the player's survival and needs
them to continue descending. Whether the network imprisoned her for her protection
or everyone else's is unresolved.

A later escalation could be her first unexpected contact aboard the ship, beyond
the limits she previously described. Another could be the CEO recognizing her
voice on an intercepted channel. Neither event settles her identity or allegiance.

Sample dialogue, not final script:

> Echo: Don't take the lift. The cables are intact. The thing beneath it isn't.

> Echo: I wanted to know you were safe.
>
> Player: How are you reaching this channel?

> CEO: Who gave you access to that channel?
>
> Echo: Disconnect. Now.

Her initial contact must work before the first relic is collected. A nearby ancient
terminal or dormant relay is the proposed channel; taking the relic could make the
connection portable. This replaces the earlier suggestion that she first speaks
only after the relic has already been recovered.

## Opening: wake up on the first world

**Agreed:** begin in the world with the player disoriented. Teach the controls in
context and build toward running through danger and recovering the first relic.
Not knowing the location does not establish total amnesia or erase the chosen
character's background.

See [opening sequence](opening.md) for the playable tutorial treatment. Exact
planet, arrival circumstances, relic function, and route to the ship remain open.

## Campaign treatment (proposal)

1. **Waking into the mystery.** Wake on an unfamiliar world, learn to survive with
   Echo's help, and recover the first relic. Reach safety and establish access to
   the proposed ship hub. Salvage work, local contacts, and discoveries then reveal
   the wider planetary network.
2. **Following the connection.** Recover components and restore local nodes.
   The corporation moves from offering payment to demanding ownership and sending
   retrieval teams. Communities gain independent access as the player helps them.
3. **The race to the center.** Restored devices reveal a missing network hub.
   The CEO has been tracing the player's activations and needs the Skavengar to
   open the last planet. Reconstruction has helped keep that effort alive.
   Discover why the network was severed and what restoring it would mean today.
4. **The confrontation.** Prevent exclusive corporate control of the network.
   Exact finale, CEO encounter, restoration consequences, and player choices are open.

The previous salvage-job/initial-employer opening is superseded by waking on the
first world. A past corporate connection remains possible backstory, not a decided
fact. The CEO's introduction need not interrupt the playable opening.

The network's shutdown should have a meaningful explanation, still to be written.
Post-campaign distant signals and harder expeditions are an endgame possibility.

## Ship and supporting cast (proposal)

The ship is home, workshop, and collection space: artifacts, specimen tanks, a
growing star chart, and ancient devices visibly record progress. A small survey
drone could accompany the player. Recurring contacts and communities give the
discoveries personal stakes. Crew size, NPC identities, and the ship layout are open.

Early story motif: keep an incomplete machine aboard. Add pieces over multiple
expeditions until it projects a star absent from modern charts.

## Planet concepts (working names)

| Destination | Identity | Finds and return hooks |
|---|---|---|
| Scrapyard Moon | Wrecks, scavenger camps, buried machinery | Salvage, weapon components, sealed ship interiors |
| Overgrown World | Alien forests consuming research stations | Specimens, living equipment, ruins beneath roots |
| Glass Desert | Crystalline dunes and exposed observatories | Energy technology, signal puzzles, storm-revealed structures |
| Dark Ocean World | Abandoned platforms above a black sea | Deep-site artifacts and late network discoveries |

Four destinations are an initial scope proposal, not a locked count or order.
Each should remain useful after its main story objective is complete. Major sites
and discoveries persist; smaller routes and encounters can vary on return visits.

## Open story questions

- Why does the player wake here, what do they remember, and how do they reach the ship?
- What is Echo's real identity and motive, and what does she withhold? Is Echo her final name?
- What does the first relic do, and why does Echo need the player to take it?
- Who were the builders, why was the network severed, and what happened to them?
- What personal motive brings each starting character into salvage exploration?
- What are the corporation's final name, visual identity, and relationship to
  other weapon manufacturers?
- Is the CEO ever revealed? What makes their final confrontation satisfying?
- Which allies, settlements, and discoveries carry the campaign's emotional stakes?
- What changes in the galaxy after the ending?
