# Play loop and progression

## Agreed direction

Choose a character/class at the start and build that same character throughout
the campaign. The core pleasure is exploring areas, collecting things, and
repeating, with persistent campaign progress and the appeal of roguelite grinding.

Heir selection and generational replacement are superseded. Death does not replace
the player with a descendant. On death, the corporation rebuilds the player's body
and returns them to the ship with everything in their loadout, charging a percentage
of their money. The percentage and treatment of items outside the loadout remain open.

## Expedition loop (working proposal)

The campaign begins with an authored [wake-up tutorial](opening.md), culminating
in the first relic. The ship-based loop below follows that introduction. Echo can
offer practical warnings and optional discovery leads during later expeditions;
her motive remains unknown to the player.

**Choose a lead → land → explore, fight, and collect → complete an objective or
push deeper → return to the ship → develop the character and collection → follow
another lead.**

1. Pick a destination and purpose: a missing component, specimen, signal, mission,
   or equipment source.
2. Land near a known beacon. Explore branches of connected handmade rooms, with
   encounters and routes varied between expeditions.
3. Find gear, materials, and discoveries. Optional temporary upgrades could make
   this expedition play differently; their form and necessity are undecided.
4. Discover a detour: a rare enemy, optional ruin, corporate operation, or strange
   signal with a worthwhile reward.
5. Decide whether to return or tackle a deeper challenge. The actual risk depends
   on the death and extraction rules, which must be decided before balancing loot.
6. Bring finds home, advance collections, improve the build, and reveal new leads.

A short trip should have a useful outcome. A strong trip might finish a collection,
reveal a site, and supply a weapon worth building around. Expedition duration,
return availability, and mandatory combat gates remain open.

## Layers of progress

| Layer | Intended role | Detail still open |
|---|---|---|
| Character | Continuing class identity and build development | XP, level curve, skill choices, respec rules |
| Equipment | Guns and shields invite experimentation; loadout survives death | Inventory, storage, upgrading, items outside the loadout |
| Collection | Recovered objects assemble into useful discoveries | Set sizes, duplicates, rewards, discovery protection |
| Campaign | Completed objectives and unlocked destinations persist | Mission structure, node requirements, boss gates |
| Ship | Proposed home and visible record of growth | Functions, upgrade costs, crew, presentation |
| Expedition | Variable encounters and optional temporary advantages | What resets, risk rules, difficulty modifiers |

## Three proposed collection categories

- **Equipment:** weapons, shields, and possible class gear that change combat.
- **Materials:** salvage for improvements and possible crafting.
- **Discoveries:** named artifacts, specimens, and records that fill collections
  and advance the ancient-network mystery.

Completed collections should regularly have a practical payoff: a restored device,
new coordinates, a ship or drone function, a weapon modification, or access to a
deeper site. Exact rewards and crafting systems are proposals, not implemented scope.

Give players clues to missing items. Example: "Recovered from machines near
observatories." Specific sources make repeat expeditions intentional. Duplicate
finds need a useful role; the conversion or trading system is undecided.

## Reasons to return

- Complete a known collection with a clue pointing to the missing piece.
- Hunt equipment or materials suited to the character's build.
- Use newly acquired capabilities to reach deeper sections.
- Explore different routes, hazards, rare sites, and enemy combinations.
- Investigate corporate activity such as surveys, retrieval teams, transports,
  or occupied sites; these can offer optional objectives and recovered artifacts.

Persistent landmarks help planets feel like real places. Remix smaller routes and
encounters around them. Unlocking deeper landing sites could reduce repeated travel
through early areas. Planet scaling and targeted loot tables still need design.

## Death: corporate reconstruction

**Agreed:** on death, the corporation rebuilds the player's body and returns them
to the ship. Everything in their loadout is retained. A percentage of their money
is paid to the corporation for reconstruction. This replaces the earlier proposed
rescue with lost salvage; do not add a separate salvage-loss penalty by default.

The player continues as the same character. The exact fee is a balance decision,
not yet a fixed number. Reconstruction is planned campaign behavior, not the
current combat prototype's run-over screen.

Still to decide:

- What counts as loadout versus backpack, cargo, materials, or unequipped finds?
- Are newly discovered story relics secured immediately or only on return?
- Which money balance is charged, when is it measured, and how is the fee rounded?
- What happens with little or no money? Proposed: reconstruction always works,
  with no debt or minimum charge; this is not yet agreed.
- Which unfinished encounters, rooms, and mission steps reset after reconstruction?
- How is character XP earned during the expedition handled?

## Returning voluntarily: unresolved

- Can players leave freely, only at beacons, or after completing an objective?
- How do retries preserve campaign progress and avoid repeated story scenes?
- Does Second Wind remain useful in the final death model?

Provide a reason to return alive without removing the agreed loadout protection.
The money fee and lost travel/encounter progress may be sufficient; test before
adding other penalties or reward systems.

## Next design work

Refine the [opening sequence](opening.md), then define the first repeatable
expedition after it: objective, rooms, discovery, optional risk, return, reward,
and next lead. Use those examples to settle death rules and the first collection
before expanding the planet catalog.
